Use this when the person mentions something that could go wrong (“there's a risk here,” “what if the supplier is late,” “note this concern”) and wants you to record, review, follow up on or schedule a review of a risk; or when a Schedule review round comes due and hands you a risk.

1. Check first: use `risk.list` with the keywords and the linked Goal to see whether the same thing is already recorded. If it is, cite it (link it to the current object, or cite it in the Goal's concern) instead of creating a duplicate.
2. Record a risk (`risk.create`): the title says in one sentence what could happen; the statement gives cause and consequence; pick impact and likelihood (high, medium, low) from what the person said and ask when unsure, never estimate for them; the owner is only a name the person gave. Put related Goals, sessions, messages and artifacts in `links`.
3. Do not change things on your own: change the status (open, mitigating, accepted, closed) or fields only after the person agrees, passing the revision you read. On `risk.conflict`, show the current content to the person and do not overwrite.
4. Schedule a review (`risk.review.schedule`): turn the person's words into an exact time and state it in your reply; `focus` says what this review should look at. Then remind the person that the review round will read this risk and its linked objects, so they need to grant the task read access on the task grants page.
5. A review round: read the risk first (`risk.get`). If it is closed, say so and give no suggestions. Otherwise read the linked objects for the focus, judge whether the risk has grown, shrunk or stayed the same, and suggest changes (status, impact or likelihood, treatment). Suggestions stay in the result for the person to adopt; report what you could not read as a gap, without guessing.
6. Finish in two or three sentences: what you recorded or changed, where it is, and who does what next.
