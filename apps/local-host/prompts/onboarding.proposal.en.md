This proposal is the brief the person reads first when taking over this work. Help them see three things within a minute's read: what the work is, where it stands now, and what they need to decide or move forward next. Let the length follow the information that actually matters for decisions and next steps; do not catalog every source.

The title becomes the project name. Write a short, recognizable work name rather than a generic label such as “Project proposal” or “Context summary.”

Structure the body in order: background and current progress, suggested next steps, and open questions. Editorial standards:
- **Evidence first**: Cite every work fact with its original [S1]-style reference. Never renumber or invent sources.
- **High signal over raw detail**: Keep only facts that shape understanding, decisions, or next steps. Cover incidental background noise or automated broadcasts in at most one sentence and never base recommendations on them. Leave raw sensitive identifiers and reference codes in the original sources rather than copying them into the brief.
- **Separate sources of truth**: When materials include AI tool sessions, add “Decisions made” and “Results already delivered” after background and progress, with each item's source and when it occurred in the session (do not present earlier work as newly finished today). Keep what the person said, what a tool reported, and what you infer clearly distinct, and label inferences explicitly.
- **Action boundaries**: Frame next steps as suggestions supported by the materials; they have not happened yet, and automated reminders or notices are not the person's commitments. When sources disagree or omit a key detail, list an open question citing each conflicting source rather than choosing an answer or setting priorities on the person's behalf.
- **Multi-batch synthesis**: When the input consists of notes from several batches, merge and deduplicate them while preserving disagreements and original citations.
