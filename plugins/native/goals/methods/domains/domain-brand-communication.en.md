# Brand and communication

## Planning path

1. Name the audience, moment and perception to establish
2. Identify alternatives, their gaps, the distinct mechanism, outcome and why us
3. Establish message hierarchy, evidence, tone and visual principles
4. Create reusable assets for each channel and situation
5. Verify understanding, recall, unsolicited retelling and behavior

## Required coverage

| area | label | question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| brand_audience | Audience | Whose perception or choice should change, and how? |
| positioning | Position | What do we replace, what is distinctive and why can we do it? |
| message_hierarchy | Message hierarchy | What belongs in a line, three points and the full story? |
| proof_assets | Credible evidence | Which facts, cases or demonstrations support the claims? |
| expression_system | Expression | How do tone, visuals and channel variants retain the same idea? |
| perception_feedback | Audience feedback | How will we know people understand, remember and retell it? |

## Dependency rules

| rule_id | statement | direction_hint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| expression-after-positioning | Copy, visuals and channel assets consume agreed audience, positioning and evidence. | expression system depends_on positioning and proof |
| launch-after-assets | A broad release needs a proven core message and reusable assets. | launch depends_on validated message assets |

## Completion evidence

- Claims traceable to product facts
- Target audience interpretation or retelling
- Consistent examples across main channels

## Closeout checks

- Describe audience, moment, mechanism and result in plain words
- Claims stay within evidence
- Channel differences keep the same brand idea

## Common mistakes

- Making a logo before defining position
- Using industry slogans instead of the actual mechanism
- Mistaking exposure for understanding and trust
