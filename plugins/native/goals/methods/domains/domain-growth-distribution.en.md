# Growth and distribution

## Planning path

1. Specify target users, moment and behavior to change
2. Identify alternatives, reachable channels and the distinct growth mechanism
3. Design acquisition, activation, retention and voluntary sharing separately
4. Set attribution, baseline and experiment stop conditions
5. Verify the loop in real channels across repeat behavior before scaling

## Required coverage

| area | label | question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| growth_audience_moment | Users and moments | For whom, when, and why is the current alternative insufficient? |
| distribution_mechanism | Distribution | How do people find it, and how do channel and product reinforce each other? |
| activation_value | Activation | What is the earliest observable moment of core value? |
| retention_loop | Retention | Which repeating value brings people back? |
| referral_loop | Sharing | What is worth sharing, and how does it reach the next suitable person? |
| growth_measurement | Measurement | What events, definitions, attribution window and stop criteria apply? |

## Dependency rules

| rule_id | statement | direction_hint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| channel-after-audience | Channel choice needs a defined audience, moment and value proposition. | channel plan depends_on audience and moment |
| scale-after-loop-proof | More spend needs evidence of acquisition, activation, retention or sharing in real channels. | scaling depends_on proven growth loop |

## Completion evidence

- Actual reach and conversion baseline
- Event definitions for activation, retention and sharing
- An experiment with a stop condition

## Closeout checks

- Growth goes beyond one-time subsidy or manual effort
- Measures track actual user value
- Channel, product and audience explain each other

## Common mistakes

- Treating growth as advertising or posting
- Counting signups without activation or retention
- Changing multiple variables without a baseline
